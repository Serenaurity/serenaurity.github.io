Thrive — Rehabilitation Data Analysis

Jan 2026 – present · Academic project

The CV describes an earlier grant-recipient versus non-recipient comparison; the supplied analysis workspace extends this scope to current-classification levels 1–6. This Ministry of Justice Kamlangjai project, through an academic advisor, builds a governed analytics and model-evaluation workflow for population-level analysis and hypothesis generation, not individual classification or decisions.

  • Context: create a reviewable, aggregate-only analysis path without exposing person-level records, identifiers, row-level scores, or unsupported causal claims.
  • Approach: apply profiling, structure and quality checks, selection and stratified screening, date/placeholder/proxy-leakage checks, a data contract, CSV-to-Parquet preparation, and a feature mart. The model comparison includes a majority baseline, multinomial regularized logistic, ordinal cumulative-link, and HistGradientBoosting methods, alongside fairness review.
  • Deliverable: produce aggregate dashboard data, six-level factor contrasts, bounded holdout driver views, a fairness audit, model card, final report, and output checks. The research remains ongoing: codebook, rule-mapping, population, and extract-date verification are still to complete; early-cohort evaluation is framed as extrapolation, while a prison holdout supports a generalization-related comparison.

Student Dropout Risk Prediction & Early Warning System

Aug – Dec 2025 · Research

A coauthored study of 20,919 undergraduate student records from Sakon Nakhon Rajabhat University, Thailand (2014–2024). The research develops staged early-warning predictions after Terms 1, 2, and 3+ from student-level academic data.

  • Context: raw course-level records were cleaned, aggregated to credit-weighted term GPA, joined with student and academic tables, and pivoted across up to eight terms.
  • Approach: feature engineering included GPA trend, variation, failure, threshold, and interaction features. Logistic regression, random forest, and XGBoost were assessed with an 80/20 student-level split and cross-validation on training data.
  • Reported Term 3 evaluation: the CV reports that the 34-feature XGBoost configuration recorded recall 0.891, F1 0.926, ROC-AUC 0.992, and PR-AUC 0.973. These are a reported evaluation, not evidence of an intervention effect.

ArguSQL

Personal application · Windows MVP

A local-first Windows SQL workspace for exploring files without treating a local dataset as a remote service. It is designed around a reviewable path from opening a file through reading results and exporting a new CSV.

  • Workflow: open local CSV, TSV, Parquet, JSON, or JSONL files → write a SELECT or CTE query → inspect the live preview → execute and page through results → export a new CSV while preserving original files.
  • Approach: Electron, React, TypeScript, Monaco, and native DuckDB support scoped completion, a visual ER/JOIN builder, profiling, reviewed Filter-to-SQL, and saved query and data tabs.
  • Deliverable: a Windows MVP with a local synthetic ecommerce demo. Remote connectors, DML/DDL, and data upload are outside its current scope.

Publication

A Machine Learning-Based Student Dropout Risk Prediction and Early Warning System

Coauthor · Accepted · 2026

Accepted for publication in Asian Health, Science and Technology Reports, 34(4), Oct–Dec 2026. The study compares logistic regression, random forest, and XGBoost across prediction stages after Terms 1, 2, and 3+.

For the reported XGBoost configuration, Term 1 used 16 features (recall 0.621, F1 0.640, ROC-AUC 0.866, PR-AUC 0.709); Term 2 used 21 features (recall 0.601, F1 0.657, ROC-AUC 0.870, PR-AUC 0.732); and Term 3 used 34 features (recall 0.891, F1 0.926, ROC-AUC 0.992, PR-AUC 0.973).

Single-institution evaluation; cross-institution validation and intervention impact remain future work. No DOI, public paper link, or preprint has been provided yet.
